# One day in Colmar: a relaxed itinerary

*The old town, lunch, a museum or a boat ride, and an evening by the water. How to spend one day in Colmar without turning it into a race.*

One day is enough to see Colmar's old town at an easy pace and add one thing you're especially interested in — as long as you don't try to do it all. A simple plan: start with a walk through the historic center, stop for lunch, then pick one activity for the afternoon, either a museum or a boat ride on the canals.

For the morning walk, try [the TouringBee audio guide](bokun). It covers 27 stops in about an hour and a half, with no tour group to keep up with. Start whenever you like.

## Your day at a glance

Treat this as a rough plan, not a fixed schedule. Shuffle things around depending on the season, the day of the week, and the weather: if you'd rather see a museum first or it's raining in the morning, start with the museum and save the walk for later.

Colmar's historic center is small, and the main sights are close together. So there's no need for a long checklist: plan a walk through the old town, lunch, and one activity you care about, and keep the evening open. If you want to fit in several museums or a trip out of town, you'll need a faster pace or more time.

The old town is best explored on foot, since much of the center is pedestrian-only. If you came by car, leave it in a parking lot for the day.

Keep the day of the week in mind: the Unterlinden Museum is closed on Tuesdays, and the covered market is closed on Mondays.

## Morning: the old town with an audio guide

The hour-and-a-half walk is the backbone of the day. The audio guide follows a set route through the old town, but you play the audio at each stop when you're ready and set your own pace. Plan on about two hours for this part of the day: you'll want to stop along the way, take photos of the facades, or grab a coffee.

If you'd like to step into a church, a museum, or the market along the way, check their opening hours ahead of time — each place keeps its own schedule.

## Lunch: mind the kitchen hours

At some restaurants, lunch service ends around 13:30–14:00, and the kitchen doesn't reopen until the evening. So don't push lunch back too late, and check the hours of the place you have in mind.

Another option is the **covered market** near Little Venice, where you can have lunch or a quick bite. It's closed on Mondays and open only until 14:00 on Sundays.

## Afternoon: pick one activity

**The Unterlinden Museum** — if you're into art. The museum is housed in a former 13th-century convent, and the most famous work in its collection is the Isenheim Altarpiece. It's usually open Wednesday through Monday, 9:00 to 18:00, and closed on Tuesdays, with some holiday exceptions.

**A canal boat ride** — if you'd like to see the town from the water. Boats leave from Saint-Pierre Bridge, the ride takes about 25 minutes, and the schedule depends on the season.

**Explore on your own** — if you'd rather skip tickets and a set plan: revisit places you liked in the morning, stop at a café, or simply wander.

There's no need to squeeze in both the museum and the boat: choose the one that appeals to you more and leave time for an unhurried walk. If you plan to visit several museums and take a boat ride, compare the total cost of the tickets with the price of the [Colmar City Pass](/things-to-do-in-colmar/).

## Evening: a walk by the water or dinner in the old town

In the evening, head back to [Little Venice](/petite-venise-colmar/) and Fishermen's Quay for a leisurely stroll by the water or dinner. If you don't feel like going back, pick a restaurant or a winstub — a traditional Alsatian tavern — in another part of the old town.

On selected Tuesdays in summer, Place de l'Ancienne Douane, the square by the old customs house, hosts free Alsatian folklore events. They're seasonal, so check the current dates before your trip.

## How to adjust the plan

- **If it's raining**, start with a museum and save the walk for when the rain lets up. If you want to stay indoors, consider the Unterlinden Museum, the Bartholdi Museum, the Toy Museum, or the covered market during its opening hours.
- **In winter**, keep the short days and museum hours in mind: plan the walk for daylight. Check the boat schedule before you go.
- **During the [Christmas market](/colmar-christmas-market/) season**, set aside time for the markets, but check their hours in advance — some close fairly early. The center gets especially crowded at this time of year.
- **If you only have half a day**, choose the old town plus one other priority: Little Venice, a museum, or a boat ride.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is one day enough for Colmar?**
One day is enough for the old town and one more activity. For several museums, nearby villages, and the Alsace Wine Route, you'll need more time.

**Where should I start my day in Colmar?**
In most cases, with a walk through the old town — afterward, it's easier to decide where to go next. If a museum is your priority or it's raining, you can switch the order.

**Can I have lunch at the covered market?**
Yes, during its opening hours. You can find places to eat or grab a snack. On Sundays it's open only until 14:00, and it's closed on Mondays.

**Can I do a day trip to Colmar from Strasbourg?**
Yes. [Direct trains](/how-to-get-to-colmar/) run between Strasbourg and Colmar, making a day trip straightforward.

## Before you go

Check the museum and covered market hours, as well as the boat schedule — they vary by day of the week and by season. Everything else you can decide on the spot, depending on the weather and your mood.
