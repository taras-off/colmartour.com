Colmar Museums: Which One to Choose

The Isenheim Altarpiece, the house of the Statue of Liberty's sculptor, Hansi's watercolors, rare old books, and museums the whole family will enjoy.

By Eugene · Updated November 2026 · 9 min read

Buy the audio guide — €9.99 At a glance

People come to Colmar for the half-timbered houses and the canals, but there's plenty to see behind museum doors, too. The Unterlinden Museum is home to the Isenheim Altarpiece. At the Bartholdi Museum, you can see early models of some of the sculptor's most famous works. There are also small museums that are easy to fit in between walks. Colmar has around a dozen museums, and most of them are in the old town, within easy walking distance of one another. You don't need to see them all. It's better to pick one or two that match your interests.

Museums at Christmas

From November 25, 2026, to January 3, 2027, the cloister of the Unterlinden Museum hosts an evening light-and-sound show, Once Upon a Night. It's a good reason to come back to the museum after an evening at Colmar's Christmas markets.

Over the holidays, museums change their hours, and some close for a few days, so check the schedule in advance if you're visiting during the last week of December.

The Unterlinden Museum: Colmar's main museum

If you only have time for one museum, make it the Unterlinden. This is a place to spend a few hours without rushing. It occupies a 13th-century Dominican convent, and the buildings themselves are worth a look. The convent rooms hold art from the Middle Ages and the Renaissance, including works by Martin Schongauer, Holbein, and Cranach. The new brick wing across the square is devoted to the 20th century, with Monet, Picasso, and Dubuffet.

Still, most people come for the Isenheim Altarpiece (1512–1516). Its painted panels once opened according to the church calendar to reveal different scenes, and today you can study them up close in the former convent chapel. The most unusual part of the story is that the Isenheim Altarpiece was made for a hospital monastery that cared for the seriously ill.

The Bartholdi Museum is a very different place. Above all, it's a home: the house on Rue des Marchands where the sculptor of the Statue of Liberty was born in 1834. It holds models of his monuments, including the original models of the Statue of Liberty and the Lion of Belfort, and the living rooms still have the family's furniture. A whole floor is devoted to his trips to the United States and to how the Statue of Liberty came to be. The museum is small and easy to combine with a walk, since you'll find traces of Bartholdi outside its walls too, from fountains in town to a replica of the Statue of Liberty at the entrance to the city.

The Hansi Museum is on Rue des Têtes, across from the House of Heads. Jean-Jacques Waltz, known as Hansi, was an artist and illustrator whose work has become part of Alsace's heritage. The museum shows Alsace through his drawings, and it's easy to fit into a walk around the center. Across about ten rooms, the exhibition traces Hansi's life from childhood to his years in exile. You'll see watercolors of Alsatian landscapes, advertising posters, and painted tableware. You can ask for a children's activity booklet at the front desk, and before you leave, stop by the museum shop.

The Dominican quarter: a church and a library

Just a short walk from the Unterlinden Museum, the Dominican Church and the Dominican Library are two quieter places worth exploring.

The Dominican Church is a Gothic church begun in 1283. Since 1973, it has housed Martin Schongauer's most important painting, the Madonna of the Rose Bower. The church isn't open all year, only in season, so check the dates before you go.

The Dominican Library is in the former monastery on the neighboring square. It reopened to the public in 2022 after restoration. Its collection includes about 1,200 manuscripts and 2,300 incunabula, books printed before 1501. It's more than a reading room. An exhibition traces the history of the book from the Middle Ages to the 18th century. Entry is free, and you can also walk through the cloister and the medieval garden.

Museums for the whole family

At the Toy Museum on Rue Vauban, you'll find toys from the 19th century to the present day, all in a former movie theater. The collection brings together toys from different generations, so visitors of all ages can spot familiar favorites and see how children's games have changed over time. After old paintings and altarpieces, it's a nice change of pace, and kids can take a break in the play area.

The Natural History and Ethnography Museum in Little Venice grew out of the collections of a learned society founded by local scholars in 1859. Today, it shows animals from Alsace and far beyond, prehistoric fossils, rare ethnographic objects, and a small collection from ancient Egypt.

If you're traveling with kids, you may enjoy mixing museum visits with a walk through the old town or a boat ride in Little Venice.

If it rains, three privately run museums share a building near Saint Martin's Collegiate Church: a chocolate museum (Choco-Story), an Alsatian wine museum, and the Wax of Legends wax museum.

How many museums can you visit?

  • If you have an hour or two, choose the Hansi Museum or stop by the Dominican Church and the library next door.
  • If you have half a day, give it to the Unterlinden Museum, then stroll through the old town.
  • If you have a whole day in Colmar, start with the Unterlinden, then visit the Bartholdi or Hansi Museum after lunch. You'll still have time for a walk.
  • On a rainy day, you can easily spend a few hours in the Unterlinden. And if you're traveling as a family, head for the Toy Museum with its play area.

Colmar City Pass

If you're planning to visit several museums, take a look at the Colmar City Pass. It covers all the paid museums in the table, the chocolate, wine, and wax museums, plus a boat ride and a ride on the little tourist train. The pass is valid for four consecutive days from first use, and you can use each attraction once. During the Christmas markets, the train ride in the pass is replaced by a walking tour with the tourist office.

Heading to Colmar?

For a short trip, one museum is enough: the Unterlinden if you have half a day, or the Hansi Museum if you have an hour. Colmar shows you the rest in its streets, where Bartholdi's works stand among the half-timbered houses.

FAQ

Which Colmar museum should I choose if I only have time for one?

The Unterlinden Museum. It's home to the Isenheim Altarpiece and a collection that runs from archaeology to 20th-century art. The tourist office suggests allowing half a day.

Which museum is best with kids?

The Toy Museum with its play area, or the Natural History Museum. At the Hansi Museum, you can ask for an activity booklet for your child.

Are Colmar museums open at Christmas?

Many museums close early on December 24 and 31, and almost all of them are closed on December 25. The Natural History Museum is closed from December 25 to 31, and the Bartholdi Museum takes a winter break in January.
