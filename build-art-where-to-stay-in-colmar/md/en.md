*Old town, Little Venice, the station or a wine village. The area you choose shapes more than the view from your window — it shapes your whole day.*

## The old town

A protected historic centre, a large pedestrian zone, houses from the Middle Ages to the 18th century. Staying here means stepping out early in the morning, before the main daytime flow of visitors, and walking home at night.

Examples: the hotel in the Maison des Têtes, a listed building on rue des Têtes, or the Mercure Colmar Centre Unterlinden next to the Unterlinden Museum.

If you're driving, ask about parking when you book. The centre is largely pedestrian, and in the Christmas season cars are kept out during market hours. More in our guide to [parking in Colmar](/colmar-parking/).

## Little Venice

The Lauch river, colourful façades, little boats — the Colmar of the postcards. Staying here means seeing the quays not just by day but early in the morning and late in the evening too.

Examples: the Hostellerie Le Maréchal in a 16th-century house on the canal, and Le Colombier Suites in an 18th-century house with a garden over the water. Just outside the quarter, a few steps away, is Maison Turenne, with paid covered secure parking — an option if you want to stay by the canals but are arriving by car.

## Near the station

The most practical base without a car. The station is only a few minutes' walk away, with trains to Strasbourg and Basel and easy connections for day trips around the region. The old town is roughly a 15-minute walk away, and a free shuttle runs around the centre (see [how to get to Colmar](/how-to-get-to-colmar/)).

Examples: The Originals City Hôtel Colmar Gare by the station, and Colmar Hôtel, with a 24-hour reception, ten minutes' walk from the old town.

## South of the centre: the German quarter

The official visitor guide calls it one of the most popular districts in modern Colmar. It was built between 1870 and 1918, when Alsace was German: villas and mansions in styles from neo-Romanesque to Art Deco, greenery, the Lauch river. It's a spacious, green residential area outside the tourist core of the old town.

It suits those staying several days who care more about greenery and a residential feel than about living inside the old town.

## The outskirts, if you're driving

If you'll be using the car every day, it's easier to stay on the edge of town. Your own parking, quick access to the motorway and the Wine Route, and you can still get into the centre by car or public transport.

Examples around the exhibition centre (Parc des Expositions): Ibis Styles Colmar Nord, with secure parking and a heated pool; Arc En Ciel, with a large free car park; B&B Colmar Expo. Just outside town in Horbourg-Wihr: L'Europe, with free parking.

## Colmar or a wine village?

Staying in a village on the Wine Route is a different kind of trip. Mornings among the vines, evenings in the village, quiet. The trade-off is getting into town: without a car, every trip to Colmar has to fit around a timetable.

Without a car, Turckheim is especially convenient: it's linked to Colmar by train and bus. Some other villages also have direct buses. Camping Le Médiéval is 500 metres from Turckheim's historic centre, and in season you can catch the traditional evening round of the night watchman; in winter he has a separate Christmas calendar.

Other examples from the official guide: in Wettolsheim, the family-run Au Soleil, 5 km from Colmar right on the Wine Route; in Kaysersberg, the Abbaye d'Alspach in a former 11th-century convent.

## If you're coming for Christmas

During the market season, people book accommodation in Colmar well ahead. If you're driving, remember that cars are kept out of the centre during market hours, so staying in the old town when you're driving means extra hassle. How the markets work is in [Colmar Christmas markets](/colmar-christmas-market/), and how to add the villages is in [Christmas markets near Colmar](/christmas-markets-near-colmar/).

## If the Wine Route is the main goal

Colmar is a convenient starting point for the Wine Route. If you're driving, stay on the outskirts with your own parking or in a wine village. Without a car, stay near the station, where trains and buses are easiest to catch. And remember: tastings and driving don't mix. More in our guide to the Wine Route [LINK: article #20].

## Hotel, apartment or guest room?

French listings use several types of accommodation, and the difference isn't always obvious. The official guide explains it like this:

| Type | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| Chambre d'hôtes (B&B) | A room in a local's home, with breakfast and linen. No more than 5 rooms and 15 guests |
| Chambre chez l'habitant | Rooms in the owner's property when more than 5 rooms are let and/or more than 15 people stay; not classed as a hotel |
| Location saisonnière / meublé de tourisme | A furnished apartment or house rented out whole for a short stay; it may hold an official classification of 1 to 5 stars |
| Tourist residence | Apartments with shared services: reception, pool, laundry |

There are budget options too: the Mittelharth youth hostel, 1 km from the historic centre, and Camping de l'Ill on the riverbank, open from late March to early January.

## Common mistakes

**Booking an old-town hotel when driving, without asking about parking.** The centre is largely pedestrian, and in the Christmas season cars are kept out during market hours.

**Staying in a village without a car and heading into Colmar every day.** It's possible, but your day will revolve around the timetable. Without a car, only pick a village once you've checked its transport links to Colmar.

**Choosing an area from a single photo.** Little Venice is beautiful, but if you plan to catch a train every morning, the station area is more practical.

**Leaving a Christmas booking to the last minute.** During the market season, accommodation is booked early.

## FAQ

**Where is it easiest to stay on a first visit?**
If you mainly want to explore the town, in the old town or near Little Venice. If you plan lots of regional trips, near the station. If you're driving, choose a hotel with its own parking.

**Where should I stay without a car?**
In the old town or near the station. The station area is more convenient if you plan regional trips by train and bus.

**Where should I stay with a car?**
On the outskirts with your own parking, for example near the exhibition centre, or at a hotel close to the centre that has its own car park.

**Should I stay in Colmar or in a wine village?**
In Colmar if the town and its museums come first. In a village if the vineyards come first and you have a car. Without a car, Turckheim is especially convenient thanks to its rail link; for other villages, check direct buses to Colmar in advance.

**Is there budget accommodation in Colmar?**
Yes: the Mittelharth youth hostel and Camping de l'Ill on the banks of the Ill.
