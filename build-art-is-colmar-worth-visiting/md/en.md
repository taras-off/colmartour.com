## What Colmar is like in practice

Colmar's historic centre is protected, much of it is pedestrian, and it's often called an open-air museum. The Maison des Têtes, the Maison Pfister, the Koïfhus, St Martin's Church — all within walking distance. For context: the tourist office's official walking trail around town is 6.1 km, about 2 hours 30 minutes, with 40 points of interest. You can see the essentials of the historic centre in less time.

Around it lies Alsace: the Wine Route, villages, castles. Colmar is a convenient starting point for all of it. So the answer to "how many days" doesn't depend on the town alone.

## Half a day: the highlights

Half a day is enough to walk the old town and Little Venice. If you have time left, add a canal boat trip or the little tourist train: a loop takes about 35 minutes.

If you try to add the Unterlinden Museum to an old-town walk in half a day, you'll have to pick up the pace: its collections span almost 7,000 years of history — from prehistory to 20th-century art, including the Isenheim Altarpiece — and it can easily take up a part of the day on its own. ## One day: the essentials, unhurried

One day is enough to see Colmar's essentials at a relaxed pace. In a day you can combine the old town, lunch and one more thing — a museum, say, or a boat trip. How to put that day together will be covered in our guide to one day in Colmar [LINK: article #11].

If you want to add villages or the Wine Route without rushing, plan an extra day.

## Why stay the night

Staying over lets you see Colmar early in the morning and in the evening, outside the busiest daytime hours.

In December an overnight stay makes particular sense: Colmar at Christmas really comes into its own after dark. For the markets themselves, see [Colmar Christmas markets](/colmar-christmas-market/); for where to stay, see [where to stay in Colmar](/where-to-stay-in-colmar/).

## Day two: Colmar as a base

From day two, Colmar works well as a base for exploring Alsace.

- **Museums.** The Unterlinden Museum, the Bartholdi Museum and others — whatever didn't fit into day one [LINK: article #14].
- **Villages.** Eguisheim, Riquewihr, Kaysersberg, Turckheim — each with its own character [LINK: article #21].
- **The Wine Route.** The Wine Route and the vineyards start right on Colmar's doorstep [LINK: article #20].
- **Castles and other trips.** Hohlandsbourg above Colmar, Haut-Koenigsbourg, the fortified town of Neuf-Brisach [LINK: article #22].

With three days you can combine the town with one or two regional trips without rushing. And for how to get here, see [how to get to Colmar](/how-to-get-to-colmar/).

## Who will love it — and who won't

**You'll love it** if you enjoy compact historic towns, architecture, Alsatian food and wine, and don't mind walking a lot.

**It may disappoint** if you're after a big city with endless things to do. Colmar is small, and during major seasonal events the historic centre can get crowded. If you want a bigger city, compare Colmar with Strasbourg [LINK: article #23].

And behind the façades lies the complex Franco-German history of Alsace. More on that in our article on why Colmar looks both French and German [LINK: article #18].

## Common mistakes

**Trying to see both the old town and the Unterlinden in detail in half a day.** You can combine them, but you'll have to speed up; for an unhurried visit, allow a full day.

**Trying to see Colmar and several wine villages in one unhurried day.** If you want to add the region, plan an extra day.

**Driving into the centre.** The old town is largely pedestrian. Where to leave the car: see [parking in Colmar](/colmar-parking/).

## FAQ

**Is Colmar worth visiting?**
Colmar suits people who enjoy compact historic towns, Alsatian architecture, food and wine. If you're after a big city with a wider range of things to do, Colmar may feel too compact — compare it with Strasbourg.

**How many days do you need in Colmar?**
Allow one day for the town's essentials. An overnight stay adds the evening and early morning; two or three days make sense if you want to add museums, villages and the Wine Route.

**Can you see Colmar in half a day?**
The old town and Little Venice, yes. There won't be much time left for museums.

**Can you do Colmar as a day trip from Strasbourg?**
Yes: Colmar has direct trains to and from Strasbourg. More in [how to get to Colmar](/how-to-get-to-colmar/).

**Colmar or Strasbourg?**
