## The old town and Little Venice

Start with Colmar's best-known historic buildings:

- **House of Heads** (Maison des Têtes) — a Renaissance house with a facade covered in sculpted heads and masks.
- **Pfister House** — an early Renaissance building with a corner oriel window and murals on its facade.
- **Koïfhus** — the old customs house and Colmar's oldest public building. Notice its colorful tiled roof.
- **Collegiate Church of St. Martin** — a Gothic church built of yellow sandstone from Rouffach.

Even if you skip the museums, there's plenty to see in the center. And if you're into art or history, it's easy to pair your walk with a visit to one of the town's museums.

In the southern part of the old town lies [Little Venice](/petite-venise-colmar/). Colorful half-timbered houses line the Lauch River, and tour boats glide along the water — it's one of Colmar's most recognizable sights. Nearby, you'll find Fishermen's Quay and the Tanners' Quarter, where tall historic houses still stand.

Beyond the medieval center, you'll see a different side of Colmar, with villas and historic townhouses in a variety of architectural styles. Many of them went up between 1870 and 1918, when Alsace was part of the German Empire. [LINK: article №18 — on "a different side of Colmar"]

## Museums and cultural sites

The **Unterlinden Museum** is one of Colmar's major museums. Housed in a former 13th-century convent, its collections span nearly 7,000 years, from prehistory to 20th-century art. Its most famous work is the Isenheim Altarpiece. The museum is closed on Tuesdays.

If you're interested in painting, the **Dominican Church** is also worth a visit. This 14th-century Gothic church, with its stained-glass windows, is home to Martin Schongauer's "Madonna of the Rose Bower".

The **Bartholdi Museum** occupies the house where the sculptor behind the Statue of Liberty was born. Inside, you'll find models created in preparation for his sculptures. You'll also spot Bartholdi's work around town: his statue of Lazarus von Schwendi, for example, stands atop one of Colmar's fountains. [LINK: article №16] The **Hansi Museum** is devoted to Jean-Jacques Waltz, the artist and illustrator known for his images of Alsace.

If you're interested in rare books and manuscripts, stop by the **Dominican Library**. Its collection includes medieval manuscripts and early printed books. Admission is free; just check the library's opening hours before you go. With kids, try the **[Toy Museum](/colmar-with-kids/)**, the **Natural History Museum**, or the **Choco-Story** chocolate museum.

If you only have time for one museum, choose by interest: Unterlinden for art, the Bartholdi Museum for the sculptor's story, and the Toy Museum if you're traveling with kids. [LINK: article №14]

### Colmar City Pass: what's included, and is it worth it?

The Colmar City Pass costs €48 and includes admission to nine museums and attractions, one boat ride, and one trip on either the white or the green tourist train. It's valid for four consecutive days from first use, but it doesn't let you skip the line. During the Christmas market season, the train ride is replaced by a walking tour run by the tourist office, which you'll need to book in advance.

Whether it pays off depends on what you plan to see. If you're going to visit several museums, take a boat ride, and ride the tourist train, add up the individual ticket prices and compare the total with the cost of the pass.

## More things to do

Near Little Venice is the covered market, where you can shop for local products and grab a bite. It's closed on Mondays and open only until 14:00 on Sundays.

Boats leave nearby from Saint-Pierre Bridge. The ride along the Lauch takes about 25 minutes and offers a different view of the historic neighborhoods. The schedule changes with the season. If you'd rather see part of the center without much walking, hop on the tourist train: the ride takes about 35 minutes, with audio commentary about the town along the way. The white and green trains run on different schedules.

While you're in Colmar, try local dishes like tarte flambée and choucroute (Alsatian sauerkraut), along with Alsace's white wines. [LINK: article №19 — on "local dishes"] Tourism materials call Colmar the capital of Alsatian wine, and it makes a convenient base for exploring the Alsace Wine Route. If you want to see the vineyards and wine villages, plan a separate outing. Colmar itself is easy to get around on foot, but you'll want to plan transportation if you're visiting several villages. [LINK: article №20 — on "Alsace Wine Route"]

## If you're short on time

With half a day, start with the old town and Little Venice. After your walk, pick one more thing: a boat ride, the covered market, or a museum. With a [full day](/one-day-in-colmar/), you can take your time at Unterlinden or another museum.

## FAQ

**What are the must-sees in Colmar?**
On a first visit, start with the old town and Little Venice. Then choose by interest: Unterlinden, a boat ride, the covered market, or other museums.

**How much is the Colmar City Pass?**
€48. It includes nine museums and attractions, one boat ride, and one tourist train ride, and it's valid for four consecutive days from first use.

**What can you do in Colmar when it rains?**
Visit Unterlinden, the Bartholdi Museum, the Toy Museum, the Natural History Museum, or Choco-Story. The covered market is another option during its opening hours.

**Can you explore Colmar on your own?**
Yes: the center is compact and mostly pedestrian-only. If you'd like a ready-made route with commentary, try a [self-guided walking tour with an audio guide](/colmar-walking-tour/).
